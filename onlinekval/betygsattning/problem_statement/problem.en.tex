\problemname{Betygsättning}
Pelle is a programming teacher at Pelle's Optimization School (PO School).
He is currently grading his students in the course Optimization $1$.

Grading works as follows.
In total there are $x$ \texttt{A} criteria, $y$ \texttt{C} criteria, and $z$ \texttt{E} criteria.
To receive the grade \texttt{E}, you must satisfy all \texttt{E} criteria.
To receive the grade \texttt{C}, you must satisfy all \texttt{C} and \texttt{E} criteria.
To receive the grade \texttt{A}, you must satisfy all \texttt{A}, \texttt{C}, and \texttt{E} criteria.

Additionally, there are two special grades.
If you satisfy all \texttt{E} criteria and at least half of the \texttt{C} criteria, you receive a \texttt{D}.
If you satisfy all \texttt{E} and \texttt{C} criteria and at least half of the \texttt{A} criteria, you receive a \texttt{B}.

Pelle finds grading very tedious and needs your help.
Write a program that takes the number of \texttt{A}, \texttt{C}, and \texttt{E} criteria a certain student has satisfied and prints the grade the student should receive.
You can assume that the student always received at least an \texttt{E} in the course.

\section*{Input}
The first line contains three integers $x, y, z$ ($1 \leq x, y, z \leq 30$), the number of \texttt{A}, \texttt{C}, and \texttt{E} criteria that exist.
The second line contains three integers $x', y', z'$ ($0 \leq x' \leq x$, $0 \leq y' \leq y$, $0 \leq z' \leq z$), the number of \texttt{A}, \texttt{C}, and \texttt{E} criteria the student has satisfied.

\section*{Output}
Print a single letter: \texttt{A}, \texttt{B}, \texttt{C}, \texttt{D}, or \texttt{E}.

\section*{Scoring}
Your solution will be tested on a set of test groups.
To earn points for a group, you must pass all test cases in that group.

\noindent
\begin{tabular}{| l | l | l |}
\hline
Group & Points & Constraints \\ \hline
$1$     & $40$        &  The grade is \texttt{A}, \texttt{C}, or \texttt{E} \\ \hline
$2$     & $60$        &  No additional constraints. \\ \hline
\end{tabular}

\section*{Explanation of Samples}
In sample $1$, the student satisfies all criteria except one \texttt{A} criterion. The student therefore receives a \texttt{B}.

In sample $2$, the student satisfies all \texttt{E} criteria but not half of the \texttt{C} criteria. The student therefore receives an \texttt{E}.
